# How the tool-use detector works

The detector (`backend/detector.py`) reads the band's wrist accelerometer and gyroscope. It runs at 416 readings/s while a tool is selected, 208 with the mic on, and 50 otherwise, and it times everything with the band's own clock. Every 0.25 s it scores the last second of motion. The main measures are: how much vibration sits above 8 Hz (30 mg gives 50 % evidence), what share of the motion energy is up there (at least 93 % at 416/s, and 75 % at 50/s), and whether the grip is steady (wrist rotation under 30 °/s). Smaller checks rule out walking rhythm and spiky taps or claps. A tool session starts only when that evidence holds for at least 2 s and its typical (median) value is strong, so brief spikes never count. Pauses under 1.5 s stay inside one session, and the start and stop times are refined to about 0.1 s from the vibration envelope. Sessions with confidence of at least 0.75 are "confirmed". Weaker ones are "unresolved" and the technician is asked to confirm. The detector never names a tool: the identity always comes from the technician's selection. The exposure it feeds is a **screening estimate, not a certified HAV measurement**. The band measures wrist motion, not hand-transmitted vibration at the tool handle (it is not ISO 5349). So points = 2 · a² · T, where a is the selected tool profile's published vibration magnitude (its source is recorded) and T is the detected trigger time (`active_s` also leaves out pauses). Validation so far: 21 synthetic tests pass, and every real band-07 recording made today, which included handling, double-taps, shaking and talking with the hands, produced zero false tool sessions. A real tool or phone-buzz recording was never captured because calibration was cut for the MVP, so the thresholds in `backend/detector_config.json` are provisional and are retuned with `tools/label_session.py` and `tools/analyze.py`.
