Why now, and who pays

Why now

The duty is already law. UK and EU employers must assess and control vibration exposure.

One band carries it all. Mic, 6-axis motion and a light, on the hand that holds the tool.

Speech becomes structure. A spoken defect becomes a work order, and code checks the asset.

Who pays

Fixed-plant maintenance. Mines, quarries, ports, bulk terminals and their contractors.

Two buyers, one record. Safety gets the exposure log; planners see who can take the next job.

Then the US. About 1.45 million workers on vibrating tools (NIOSH), and OSHA sets no limit.

Why now. The duty already exists: UK and EU employers must assess and control hand-arm vibration, so the budget line exists. One band now carries a mic, 6-axis motion and a light, on the hand that holds the tool. And language models turn a spoken defect into a structured work order, which is safe because code checks the asset. Who pays: fixed-plant maintenance teams at mines, quarries, ports and bulk terminals, and the contractors who service them. Two buyers share one record: safety gets the exposure log, and planners see who can take the next job. Then the US, where NIOSH estimated about 1.45 million workers on vibrating tools and OSHA sets no limit.
